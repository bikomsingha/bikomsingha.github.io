About meVeterinarian, field researcher and problem-solver

I am a veterinarian (DVM, HSTU) and microbiologist (M.Sc., Bangladesh Agricultural University) working where animal health, food safety and public health meet. My training covers farm-animal medicine and clinical practice, molecular diagnostics in the laboratory, and statistical analysis of field data, so I can follow a disease problem from the farm to the bench to the results.

Research experience. For my M.Sc. I led an outbreak-based study of Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR) in goats: planning and carrying out field sampling across Mymensingh, RNA extraction, RT-PCR, competitive ELISA, and risk-factor modelling with GEE in R. The thesis was graded A+, presented as an oral talk at the BSVER conference, and is now a submitted first-author manuscript. Internships at the Central Disease Investigation Laboratory, the Livestock Research Institute and the Bangladesh Livestock Research Institute gave me hands-on experience in diagnostics, vaccine production and outbreak investigation, and I have co-authored work on antimicrobial use, poultry disease surveillance and dairy cattle genetics.

Problem-solving and new skills. I enjoy turning field problems into practical solutions, and I am learning machine learning in Python to bring data-driven tools into animal health.

Motivation and dedication. I work steadily, independently and to deadlines. When my first PCR runs failed, I troubleshot the protocol until it worked. Alongside my research I have built a Top Rated freelance career in medical writing and translation (84 completed projects) and helped pilot a food safety consultancy, which sharpened my communication and project management.

I am now looking for a funded MSc or PhD in livestock health, animal physiology and welfare, One Health or infectious disease, where I can add qPCR, sequencing and imaging methods to my field and laboratory skills.
